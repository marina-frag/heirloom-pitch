Inherited a field? Know what it's worth growing.

Heirloom gives people who inherit farmland a clear, field-specific answer: which crop pays, what it costs, how much work it takes, or whether leasing is smarter. Behind each answer is a simulation calibrated by a low-cost digital twin of the field.

Greek farmland is changing hands to people who have never farmed

Land in Greece passes mostly through inheritance. Heirs grow up in cities and face costly questions with no field-specific answers.

7.2%of Greek farm managers are under 40, one of the lowest shares in the EU1
~40%of Greek farm managers are over 651
0.7%of Greek farm managers have full agricultural training3
~85%of Greece's freshwater goes to irrigation, amid repeated drought years4

What a new owner actually asks:

  • Is this field worth farming at all, or should I lease it?
  • Which crop pays on this soil and climate?
  • What did it grow last year, and how does that change my choice?
  • How many hours, how much water and fertilizer, how much money?

From a pin on a map to a ranked, costed plan

  1. Locate and reconstruct

    The owner drops a pin on the plot. Historical Sentinel-2 satellite imagery (archive from 2015) and owner input rebuild what the field grew in past seasons.

  2. Simulate every option

    Validated open crop models (DSSAT, FAO AquaCrop) simulate yield, water and nitrogen for candidate crops and multi-year rotations. An economic layer adds prices, CAP subsidies, input and labour costs.

  3. Keep the twin in sync

    Low-cost LoRa sensors calibrate the model to the real soil before planting, then drive irrigation timing and disease-risk alerts once the crop is in.

A field network that costs hundreds, not thousands

Every node runs on its own STM32WL (RAK3172) radio module and sleeps most of the time, so batteries last for years. One gateway covers fields kilometres apart.

LoRa radio, 2–10 km in open fields 4G / internet Data packet

Soil node ×6

RAK3172 (STM32WL + LoRa), capacitive moisture at two depths, DS18B20 soil temperature, 18650 cell with 1 W solar, ASA-printed enclosure. Two per field.

€20–35 each

Weather node ×1

RAK3172 with air temperature and humidity (SHT31), leaf wetness, tipping-bucket rain gauge. Shared by neighbouring fields, because weather barely changes plot to plot.

€80–150

Gateway

Raspberry Pi with an SX1302 LoRaWAN concentrator. Adds a 4G modem where the field has no internet. Receives every packet and forwards it.

~€150

Server

ChirpStack network server, InfluxDB time series, Grafana dashboards and the DSSAT / AquaCrop simulations, in Docker on our existing Proxmox workstation.

€0 for the pilot
Three-field pilot≈ €450–500

We start one step earlier than precision agriculture

Existing tools help experienced farmers optimise a crop they already grow. We answer the question before that, for people with no farming background.

Who uses it

Primary
Landowners without farming experience, mainly urban heirs deciding what to do with a field.
Secondary
Small farmers and lessees who work this land and want lower water, energy and input costs.
Channels
Cooperatives sharing weather nodes and gateways, agronomists, local farmers looking for land to lease.

How we earn

  • Field report One-off simulation and ranked plan for a plot.
  • Twin subscription Sensor kit plus monitoring, irrigation and disease alerts.
  • Match-making Fee for connecting owners with lessees, contractors and agronomists.
